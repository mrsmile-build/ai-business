How to price your service in Nigeria without apologizing

By Elijah · founder, AI Business · no fluff, no luck, just what actually worked

Most Nigerian small-business owners under-price their own work. Not because the market won't pay more. Because somewhere in the pitch, the discount happens before the customer even asks for it. The apology is built in, and the margin is what dies.

Charging what you're worth is not greed. It is the price of being able to keep doing the work. A business that cannot afford itself has two outcomes: the owner burns out, or the quality collapses. Both are worse for the customer than a fair price.

The story behind this lesson

I watched the apology happen live, on a real deal. A service business quoted a price, the client pushed back, and before any real negotiation happened the price dropped twice — once unprompted, once on a silence the client did not actually hold. By the end, the business had won a customer who paid less than the job cost to deliver, and the resentment began before the first invoice.

The apology was not in the numbers. It was in the reflex. So I built the Proposal Generator to remove the reflex: the price you type in is the price the client sees, and there is no "discount" field because discounts are a conversation, not a template. If you want to offer one, you say so in words, and you have the dignity of deciding, not the pressure of a pre-baked slot.

Here are the five rules I actually use, in order:

1. Price the outcome, not the hour. "5 hours of work" invites a calculator. "A website that books you three new clients a month" invites a value conversation. The first gets compared to a freelancer's rate. The second gets compared to what a new client is worth to them.

2. Round numbers win trust. ₦47,500 reads like you made it up on the spot. ₦50,000 reads like you've done this before and know what it costs. Round is confident. Fractional is nervous.

3. Put the price in writing before the call. The proposal goes first, and the call is about the proposal — not about discovering what you charge mid-conversation, when your voice is already trying to justify itself.

4. Never discount without asking for something back. "I'll bring it down to ₦40,000" is a gift. "I'll bring it down to ₦40,000 if we pay upfront today" is a trade. The first makes you small. The second makes you a negotiator.

5. Track revenue like you track leads. You already count how many people enquire. Count how much they pay. The moment revenue is a number on a screen, under-pricing becomes visible to you — and visible problems get solved.

Do this now (10 minutes)
