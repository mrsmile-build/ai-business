The first sale happens before the product is finished

By Elijah · founder, AI Business · no fluff, no luck, just what actually worked

Most people build first and sell after. They polish the logo, perfect the features, wait until it's "ready" — and then discover, too late, that nobody was waiting. That order is backwards, and it's the single most expensive mistake a small business makes.

Find one buyer before you build one feature. A product without a buyer is a hobby. A buyer without a product is a lead. The conversation comes first — always.

The story behind this lesson

I spent months building AI Business in silence, convinced it had to be perfect before anyone saw it. The moment it became real wasn't a launch. It was one person — a student with exams and no time — testing it and sending me a long, honest list of what was broken. Her complaints were worth more than any feature I'd have added alone, because they were evidence someone cared enough to try.

And the first partner who said yes didn't say yes to my feature list. He said yes to a 2-minute demo link he could watch without signing up. The list got silence; the demo got a "yes, let's partner." Same product, same week. The difference was putting something in front of a human before it was finished.

Here's the evergreen truth people skip: you don't need permission to start selling, and you don't need perfection to start learning. A rough thing in front of a real person teaches you more in an afternoon than a perfect thing in front of nobody teaches you in a year.

Do this now (10 minutes)

Open your Lead Finder. Search your niche. Pick one business that went quiet 3+ days ago. Send the drafted follow-up — short, specific, human. Don't pitch your whole story; ask one question they can answer in one line.

That's it. One real conversation = one real data point. Do ten of those and you'll know more about your market than any business plan tells you.
