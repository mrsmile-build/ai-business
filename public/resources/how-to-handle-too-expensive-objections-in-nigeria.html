How to Handle Too Expensive Objections in Nigeria

How to Handle Too Expensive Objections in Nigeria

“That’s too pricey for us.” – you’ve probably heard this line more times than you can count. In Nigeria’s fast‑growing but price‑sensitive market, the “too expensive” objection can feel like a wall. The good news? It’s not an impassable barrier; it’s a conversation starter. In this guide we’ll break down why Nigerian buyers balk at price, what psychology drives the objection, and, most importantly, actionable, battle‑tested tactics you can use today to turn “too expensive” into “just right.”

---

1. Understanding the Nigerian Pricing Landscape

1.1 Economic Realities

Nigeria is Africa’s largest economy, but it also wrestles with high inflation (averaging 18‑20% in recent years) and a fluctuating Naira. When the cost of basic goods spikes, even entrepreneurs and mid‑market firms tighten their belts. According to a 2023 World Bank report, over 45% of Nigerian SMEs report cash‑flow constraints as their biggest hurdle. This macro environment means price is always top‑of‑mind.

1.2 Cultural Nuances

  • Value‑Driven Buying: Nigerians love a good deal, but they also respect brands that deliver real value. The phrase “price is right” isn’t just about cheapness; it’s about perceived benefit.
  • Community Influence: Word‑of‑mouth spreads faster than any ad. If a peer says a product is “overpriced,” that sentiment can ripple through a whole network.
  • Payment Preferences: Cash‑on‑delivery still dominates, but digital wallets like Paystack (https://paystack.com/ and Flutterwave (https://flutterwave.com/ are gaining trust, especially when they offer instalment plans.

1.3 Competitive Benchmarking

Before you even launch a pricing conversation, map the competition:

1. Direct competitors – what are they charging?

2. Indirect alternatives – can the buyer DIY or use a cheaper local solution?

3. International benchmarks – are you priced higher because you import or add premium services?

Having this data on hand lets you pre‑empt the “too expensive” objection with factual context.

---

2. The Psychology Behind the “Too Expensive” Objection

2.1 Anchoring Effect

People tend to judge price against the first number they hear. If you open with ₦500,000, a later offer of ₦300,000 feels like a bargain, even if the market price is ₦250,000. Use anchoring deliberately.

2.2 Loss Aversion

Nigerians, like most humans, fear loss more than they crave gain. A price tag can feel like a loss of cash that could be used elsewhere. Counter this by emphasizing what they gain – time saved, revenue increased, risk mitigated.

2.3 Social Proof & Authority

When a respected peer or industry leader endorses a price, the objection melts away. Leverage case studies, testimonials, and even third‑party certifications (e.g., Cloudflare security badge – https://www.cloudflare.com/ to create authority.

---

3. Proven Sales Techniques to Counter Price Resistance

3.1 The “Cost‑of‑Inaction” Drill

Ask the prospect: “What will it cost you if you don’t solve this problem today?” Quantify lost revenue, extra labor, or opportunity cost. Often the cost of inaction dwarfs the price you’re asking for.

3.2 Break‑Down the ROI

Create a simple ROI calculator:

```

ROI = (Annual Revenue Increase – Annual Cost of Solution) / Annual Cost of Solution

```

If a SaaS platform costs ₦150,000 per year and generates ₦600,000 in additional sales, the ROI is 300%. Show the numbers in a clean table, perhaps hosted on a site running on Vercel (https://vercel.com/ for fast load times.

3.3 Tiered Pricing & A La Carte Options

Instead of a single, high‑ticket price, present Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3 packages. This gives the buyer a sense of control and allows them to self‑select the level they can afford. Include a pay‑as‑you‑grow plan that scales with usage – a model popular with Nigerian fintechs.

3.4 The “Feel‑Good” Guarantee

Offer a risk‑reversal: a 30‑day money‑back guarantee or a performance‑based clause (pay only when you hit X metric). This reduces perceived risk and shifts focus from price to results.

3.5 Use Local Currency & Payment Flexibility

Quote prices in Naira, not dollars, to avoid conversion anxiety. Pair this with flexible payment options:

  • Paystack or Flutterwave for instant card/pay‑mobile transactions.
  • Bank transfer with split instalments (e.g., 3‑month payment plan).
  • Mobile money via USSD for customers without bank accounts.

5. Actionable Takeaway: A Step‑by‑Step Script to Defuse “Too Expensive”

1. Acknowledge – “I completely understand price is a big factor for you.”

2. Probe – “Can you share what budget you had in mind and why that number feels comfortable?”

3. Quantify Pain – “If you continue with the current process, you’re losing roughly ₦X each month.”

4. Present ROI – Show a simple table:

```markdown

| Metric | Current (Monthly) | With Our Solution (Monthly) |

|----------------------|-------------------|-----------------------------|

| Revenue | ₦500,000 | ₦800,000 |

| Operational Cost | ₦200,000 | ₦120,000 |

| Net Gain | ₦300,000 | ₦680,000 |

```

5. Offer Flexibility – “We can start with the Core package at ₦150,000 and upgrade as you see results, or split the payment into three instalments via Paystack.”

6. Close with Guarantee – “If you don’t see at least a 20% uplift in the first 60 days, we’ll refund 100% of the fee.”

7. Ask for Commitment – “Shall we get the agreement signed and set up your onboarding call tomorrow?”

Follow this script in a live call or written proposal, and you’ll see the “too expensive” objection morph into a budget discussion.

---

Conclusion: Turn Objections into Opportunities

The “too expensive” objection is less a dead‑end and more a signal that the buyer needs more clarity on value, risk, and payment flexibility. By:

  • Understanding Nigeria’s economic and cultural context,
  • Leveraging psychology (anchoring, loss aversion, social proof),
  • Deploying proven sales techniques (cost‑of‑inaction, ROI, tiered pricing),
  • Using trusted local tools (Paystack, Flutterwave, Cloudflare, Vercel, Render, Namecheap),
  • And following a concrete, empathetic script—you’ll convert price‑resistant prospects into loyal customers.
