Your First Paying Client, With No Portfolio

The lie you've been told

"You need a portfolio to get clients, and clients to get a portfolio." It sounds like a trap, so people freeze. It's not a trap, it's a sequence — and the first step doesn't need a client at all. It needs one documented real job.

The four moves

MoveWhat you actually do
1. Do one real job, cheap or free, and document itPick a real business with a real small problem. Fix it. Photograph / screenshot before and after. That's your portfolio: one true thing.
2. Turn the result into one line of proof"Redid Mama T's menu photos; her orders from status doubled that week." One line, one number, one name (with permission).
3. Pitch the next one with the line, not with adjectivesMessage five similar businesses: the line + what you'd do for them + a small clear price. Proof first, promise second.
4. Lock the yes in writing the same hourWho, what, how much, when paid, when delivered. A verbal yes evaporates; a written one holds.

Why the small price matters

Charge something, even small, from client #2 onward. Free work teaches clients that your time is free; a small price teaches them it isn't, and filters out the people who were never going to pay. The first paid naira is worth more than ten free projects.

You don't need a portfolio to start. You need one documented real job. The portfolio is just what that job leaves behind.
