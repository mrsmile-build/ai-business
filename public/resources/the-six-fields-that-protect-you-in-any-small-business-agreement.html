The Six Fields That Protect You

Big contracts scare people. Six fields don't.

The myth is that a real agreement needs pages of legal language. It doesn't. For most small-business deals, six plain fields cover everything that actually goes wrong — and because they're short, the client signs without flinching. Fill these and you're protected; skip them and you're relying on memory.

The six, in order

#FieldWhat it stops
1Who — provider and client, full names"I thought you were working with someone else"
2What — the exact work, in one or two linesScope creep and "I also expected…"
3How much — the agreed amountThe price-memory game later
4When paid — before / half / on delivery"I'll pay you when I'm ready"
5When delivered — the date or milestoneOpen-ended "soon" that never lands
6What if it breaks — the penalty / exitThe awkward, undefined fallout

The optional seventh that saves friendships

One line of "any other terms" — a deposit, a revision count, a cancellation notice — catches the specifics your particular deal needs. Keep it to a sentence. The power is in the completeness of the six, not the length of the prose.

A good agreement is short enough that both people actually read it. If it's long enough that nobody does, it protects no one.
