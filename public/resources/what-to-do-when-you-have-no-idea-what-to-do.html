What to Do When You Have No Idea What to Do

Zero is a starting point, not a verdict

The most paralyzing place to be isn't failure — it's blankness. No dream, no skill you'd brag about, no plan, just a phone and a feeling that everyone else got a manual you missed. So you wait for clarity to arrive. It never does, because clarity is a result of moving, not a prerequisite for it.

Here is the kinder truth: you don't need to know your purpose. You need to know your constraints. Purpose is infinite and freezing. Constraints are finite and actionable. "I have ₦0, one hour a day, and I hate being on my feet" tells you more than "find your passion" ever will.

The three questions that break the freeze

Answer these in one line each. Don't think, just write the first true thing.

QuestionWhy it works
What am I unwilling to do?Negatives are easier than positives. Crossing off "office job, driving, selling to strangers" narrows the field fast — and what's left is your real starting zone.
What do I already do without thinking?The thing that's easy for you is often the thing others pay for. You don't count it because it's fluent. That's exactly why it's valuable.
Who is already around me?Your first market isn't strangers. It's the people in your contacts, your street, your group. Direction starts from proximity, not inspiration.

Then pick the smallest next move, not the big plan

You will not think your way into a life. You'll act your way into one, in increments so small they feel silly. Send one message. Do one free sample. Ask one person what they struggle with. The point isn't the money yet — it's motion, because motion creates information, and information is what clarity is made of. A person who has done three small things knows more than a person who has dreamed for three years.

You don't need a dream to start. You need a constraint and one small move. The dream is what you'll be able to see once you're walking — never while you're standing still.
