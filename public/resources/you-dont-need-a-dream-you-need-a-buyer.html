You Don't Need a Dream. You Need a Buyer.

The lie that keeps good people stuck

Somewhere along the way we were sold a story: first find your passion, your purpose, your thing — then the money follows. So millions of capable, hardworking people sit waiting to feel ready. Waiting for the dream to arrive. Waiting for clarity. And the waiting becomes the life.

Here is the truer, kinder story: you don't need a dream. You need a buyer. One person willing to pay you for the thing you already know how to do. Not a grand purpose. A transaction. A naira. Proof that the skill inside you — the one you don't even count as a skill — is worth something to someone else.

A product without a buyer is a hobby. A buyer without a product is a lead. The conversation comes first — always. The dream catches up after the first sale. It never comes before, and waiting for it is how good people stay lost for years.

Why the dream-first order is backwards

Dreams are expensive. They need capital, confidence, a plan, a brand — all the things you only build after you've eaten. Buyers are cheap. A buyer needs one thing from you: that you solve a small problem for them, today, for a price they can pay. You already have that. You've had it the whole time. You just never pointed it at a person.

The lost person doesn't need meaning. They need momentum. And momentum starts with one yes. So the order is: find a buyer → make a sale → feel the proof → then the dream grows, because now it's built on something real instead of something wished for.

The ladder that doesn't tax your poverty

This is why the tool you're reading about starts free and stays free until you've already won. You shouldn't pay to find your first customer — that's the one thing a broke person can't afford and the one thing they most need. The rule is simple: we help you make it, then we make it. Free until you benefit. Commission so the person who made their first sale can earn by sending the next lost soul their way. A price only when the tool is making you more than it costs — a receipt for a result, never a toll at the door.

Start today, not when you're ready

Pick the one thing you can do this week — braiding, writing, fixing, cooking, designing, driving, teaching. Find one person who needs it. Send the message. That's the whole secret. The rest is just repetition until the repetition becomes a business, and the business becomes a life you recognize.

You are not behind. You are not dreamless. You are one buyer away from the thing you'll later call your purpose. Go find the buyer. The dream will catch up.
