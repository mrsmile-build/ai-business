Are you scaling revenue or scaling losses? A 2-minute profit check

A busy month with no money left at the end is not growth. It is a faster treadmill. The most expensive mistake a small business makes is confusing "lots of work" with "lots of profit" - and the only way to tell them apart is to do two minutes of arithmetic most owners never do, because they are too busy serving the customer in front of them.

Check 1: What must you make today before you earn anything?

Add up what it costs you to be open for a day: transport to get goods or get to work, data, electricity or fuel, the raw material or stock you used, and anything you owe. That total is your break-even for the day. Every naira above it is profit; every naira below it is you paying to work. If you have never calculated this, you have been guessing whether today was a good day - and guessing is how a hard worker ends the month exhausted and flat.

Check 2: Are you eating your capital?

Split tonight's takings into two piles before you spend a single naira: the money that replaces what you bought (capital - do not touch it, it is tomorrow's stock) and the money that is actually yours (profit - this is what you can feed your family with). The quiet killer of small businesses is spending from the combined pile, so the "profit" you enjoyed last week was really next week's stock, and the hole grows every month you do not notice. A notebook can hold the two piles; only a system that totals them for you stops you from mixing them by accident.

Check 3: What are you owed, and who has not paid?

Revenue you have not collected is not revenue - it is a promise, and promises fade. List every invoice or balance still outstanding, with the name and the date. If you cannot, that is a leak as real as the quiet customers: work you already did, money that is quietly evaporating because nobody chased it politely. The fix is not a harder heart - it is a drafted reminder in your voice that waits for you to send it, so chasing money stops feeling like begging.

Scaling revenue vs scaling losses

Now the question the busy season never asks. If you doubled your orders tomorrow, would your profit double too - or would your costs double faster? A business that is losing money per sale scales its losses when it scales its volume. A business that knows its break-even and protects its capital scales its profit. The difference is not effort. It is whether you can see the number before you scale it.
