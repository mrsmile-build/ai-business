"Let's Just Do It, No Paperwork"

The objection is cultural, not personal

In a relationship-first market, asking for a signature can feel like importing cold corporate stiffness into a warm human exchange. The client isn't trying to scam you — they're protecting the feeling of the deal, the trust that says "we don't need all that between us." If you respond with a 12-page contract, you lose the warmth and the deal. The skill is to keep the warmth and still get the terms confirmed.

Replace "paperwork" with "a quick summary"

The words matter. "Agreement," "contract," "terms" sound heavy. "A quick summary so we both know what's what" sounds like help. Same content, different temperature:

Cold framingWarm framing
"Please sign this agreement.""Let me send a quick summary so we're both clear — one minute."
"These are my terms and conditions.""Just so nothing gets mixed up later, here's what we agreed."
"I need this in writing.""I put everything in a short note so I don't drop anything on your behalf."

The "for you" move

Make the summary feel like a service to them: "This is so you have a record of exactly what you're getting, in case anything's unclear later." When the paper is framed as their receipt and their protection, the warmth survives — because you're not demanding trust, you're offering organisation. The client who said "no paperwork" will usually say "ah, okay, send it" to a one-line summary that reads as care.
