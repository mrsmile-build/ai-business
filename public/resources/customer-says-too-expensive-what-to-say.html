A Customer Says "It's Too Expensive" — What to Actually Say

"Too expensive" is almost never about the number

When a customer says it's too expensive, they usually mean "I don't yet see why it's worth that." It is a request for proof, not a rejection. The mistake owners make is hearing "no" and cutting the price before the value has been shown. Once you discount in that moment, you have taught them that your first price was a bluff.

So the move is not to lower the number. It is to raise the visibility of what the number buys. Lesson 8 covers how to set a price you don't apologize for; this guide is what to do in the conversation when they push back on it.

The one rule that survives every negotiation

Never give something for nothing. If you must move, move the scope, the payment plan, or the timeline — and ask for something back (a deposit today, a referral, a review, a tighter deadline you can actually hit). A discount with no trade is a gift; a discount with a trade is a negotiation, and negotiations leave both sides respecting each other.
