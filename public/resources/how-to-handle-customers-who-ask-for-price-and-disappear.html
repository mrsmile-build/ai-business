How to Handle Customers Who Ask for Price and Disappear

Introduction

You've spent time crafting a compelling proposal, sent a crystal‑clear quote, and then… silence. The prospect asked for price, thanked you, and vanished. This scenario—often called price ghosting—is frustrating, but it also hides a goldmine of insight about buyer psychology and your sales process. In this article we’ll dissect why prospects disappear after receiving a price, and give you a step‑by‑step playbook to win them back, improve conversion rates, and ultimately boost revenue for your SaaS or digital‑services business.

---

Understanding the “Price Ghost” Phenomenon

When a prospect asks for a price, they are typically at the evaluation stage of the buyer’s journey. At this point they have:

1. Validated the problem – they know they need a solution.

2. Scouted the market – they are comparing alternatives.

3. Budget constraints – the price will determine if they can move forward.

If the quote lands in a gray area—too high, too low, or unclear—it triggers a cognitive bias known as the price‑anchor effect. The buyer may mentally file the quote away and shift focus to the next vendor that appears to offer a better deal. In many cases the disappearance isn’t personal; it’s a protective reflex against perceived risk.

Understanding this mental backdrop helps you frame follow‑up messaging that feels supportive rather than pushy.

---

Diagnose Why Prospects Vanish After a Quote

Before you fire off a generic reminder, take a moment to diagnose the root cause. Common reasons include:

| Reason | Symptoms | What to Look For |

|--------|----------|------------------|

| Price too high | No response after 48‑72 hrs | Compare against competitor pricing data. |

| Unclear ROI | Prospect asks follow‑up questions about value | Highlight case studies or calculators. |

| Decision‑maker bottleneck | “I need to run this by my team” then silence | Identify stakeholders early in the funnel. |

| Budget cycle mismatch | Disappears around month‑end or quarter‑end | Align outreach with fiscal calendars. |

| Communication fatigue | Multiple emails ignored | Reduce cadence, switch channel (e.g., SMS or LinkedIn). |

A quick audit of your CRM notes, email open rates, and any click‑through data can reveal patterns. If you notice that 70% of ghosted quotes happen after the second email, it signals you need a different follow‑up cadence.

---

Proactive Follow‑Up Frameworks That Convert

1. The “Value Reminder” Email (Day 2‑3)

Subject: Your ROI in 30 Days – Quick Look

Body: Re‑state the problem, show a one‑line ROI estimate, and attach a short 2‑page case study. Keep it under 150 words.

2. The “Social Proof” Nudge (Day 5‑6)

Share a video testimonial from a client in the same industry. Videos increase click‑through by ~45% according to HubSpot data.

3. The “Limited‑Time Incentive” (Day 9‑10)

Offer a 10 % discount or an extra month of support, but make it time‑bound (48‑hour window). Scarcity triggers action.

4. The “Ask‑If‑Anything‑Else” Check‑In (Day 14)

A short, polite line: “Just checking—do you have any unanswered questions about the proposal? I’m happy to clarify.” This opens the door without pressuring.

5. The “Re‑Engagement Loop” (Day 21‑30)

If there’s still silence, move the prospect to a nurture sequence: monthly newsletters, product updates, or invite them to a free webinar. This keeps your brand top‑of‑mind for future buying cycles.

By layering these touchpoints, you increase the odds of a reply from 15 % to over 35 %.

Turning the Experience into a Sales Advantage

Every ghosted quote is a learning opportunity. Capture insights in a post‑mortem template:

1. Quote Amount – Was it above the industry median?

2. Response Time – How quickly did the prospect ask for price?

3. Follow‑Up Cadence – Which touchpoint generated the highest reply rate?

4. Channel Effectiveness – Email vs. LinkedIn vs. SMS.

5. Outcome – Closed, lost, or re‑engaged later.

Analyze this data monthly. If you discover that prospects who receive a price breakdown (line‑item costs) are 20 % more likely to respond, embed that level of detail in future proposals.

Also, re‑package the ghosted leads into a segmented list for targeted educational campaigns. For instance, a drip series titled "Understanding SaaS Pricing: Why It’s Worth the Investment" can nurture them until their budget cycle aligns.

By treating ghosting as a feedback loop, you continuously refine pricing strategy, messaging, and the overall buyer experience—turning what feels like a loss into a strategic win.
