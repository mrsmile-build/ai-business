How to Manage Delivery Enquiries for a Logistics Business

Running a logistics company means juggling countless moving parts—fleet coordination, warehouse operations, customs paperwork, and, crucially, the flood of delivery enquiries that pour in from customers, partners, and internal teams. When those enquiries are mishandled, you lose revenue, damage brand reputation, and waste precious time. In this guide we’ll walk you through a step‑by‑step framework that transforms chaotic inboxes into a streamlined, data‑driven enquiry‑management engine. The methods work for small‑scale couriers and large freight forwarders alike, and they integrate easily with tools you may already be using, such as [Paystack](https://paystack.com/ [Flutterwave](https://flutterwave.com/ or cloud platforms like [Vercel](https://vercel.com/ and [Render](https://render.com/

---

1. Centralize Enquiries with a Unified Ticketing System

The first rule of effective enquiry handling is single‑source truth. Whether a customer texts, emails, chats on WhatsApp, or calls your support line, every interaction should land in one place. A ticketing platform such as Zendesk, Freshdesk, or HubSpot Service Hub creates a unique ticket ID, timestamps the request, and attaches all follow‑up messages automatically.

Why it matters

  • Visibility: Managers can see the full pipeline at a glance, preventing duplicate work.
  • Accountability: Each ticket is assigned to an agent, with SLA timers that ensure timely responses.
  • History: Future enquiries from the same client pull up past tickets, giving agents context without digging through archives.

Implementation tip: Use the platform’s email‑forwarding rule to pipe inbound emails (e.g., support@yourlogistics.com) directly into the ticket queue. For phone‑based enquiries, integrate a VoIP provider like Twilio to log call recordings as ticket attachments. This creates a truly omnichannel inbox.

---

2. Automate Routing and Prioritization

Manually assigning tickets is a recipe for bottlenecks. Modern ticketing systems provide automation rules (or “triggers”) that evaluate ticket content and route it based on predefined criteria:

  • Geography – If the enquiry mentions “Lagos”, forward it to the West‑Africa operations team.
  • Urgency keywords – Words like delayed, missing, or urgent raise the ticket priority to High.
  • Service type – Separate freight, last‑mile, and customs queries into distinct queues.

You can also connect a CRM (e.g., HubSpot or Salesforce) so that high‑value customers automatically receive a “VIP” tag, guaranteeing faster handling.

Automation tools

  • Zapier or Make (Integromat): Link your ticketing system with Google Sheets, Slack, or a custom webhook to push notifications to the right team instantly.
  • Cloudflare Workers: If you host your own enquiry portal, a lightweight Cloudflare Worker script can pre‑validate form fields and push the data to the ticketing API before the user even hits submit, reducing spam and human error.

---

3. Leverage Real‑Time Communication Channels

While ticketing provides structure, customers often expect instant answers. Integrating live chat, WhatsApp Business API, and SMS gateways alongside your ticket system creates a hybrid model where the first touch is real‑time, and the conversation is archived as a ticket for follow‑up.

a) Live Chat on Your Website

Deploy a chat widget from Intercom, Drift, or the open‑source Chatwoot. When a visitor clicks “Track my shipment”, the chat can pull data from your tracking API (hosted on Vercel or Render) and display it instantly. If the issue escalates, the chat transcript is automatically converted into a ticket.

b) WhatsApp Business API

Nigeria’s e‑commerce market heavily uses WhatsApp. By registering a WhatsApp Business number and linking it via a provider such as 360dialog, you can receive delivery enquiries directly in your ticketing dashboard. The API also supports template messages for sending shipment confirmations or payment links through Paystack or Flutterwave.

c) SMS Notifications

For drivers on the road who may not have data access, an SMS gateway (e.g., Twilio, Nexmo) can push short status updates. When a driver replies with a problem code, a webhook creates a low‑priority ticket for the dispatch team to resolve later.

---

4. Track Performance with Analytics & KPIs

Having a system is only half the battle; you must measure its effectiveness. Most ticketing platforms offer built‑in dashboards, but for deeper insight you can export data to Google Data Studio, Power BI, or an open‑source Metabase instance.

Key performance indicators to monitor:

1. First Response Time (FRT) – Target < 15 minutes for high‑priority tickets.

2. Resolution Time – Average time to close a delivery enquiry; aim for < 4 hours.

3. Ticket Volume by Channel – Identify which channels generate the most traffic (WhatsApp vs. email) and allocate resources accordingly.

4. Customer Satisfaction (CSAT) – Send a short post‑resolution survey via SurveyMonkey or Typeform; a score above 85% indicates healthy service.

5. Repeat Enquiry Rate – High repeat rates signal systemic issues (e.g., unclear tracking information) that need process improvement.

By linking ticket data with your ERP or TMS (Transport Management System), you can also spot operational patterns—like a spike in “delivery delayed” tickets after a particular route change—allowing you to proactively adjust schedules.

---

5. Build a Knowledge Base & Self‑Service Portal

The most cost‑effective way to reduce inbound enquiries is to empower customers to help themselves. A well‑structured knowledge base answers the most common questions—tracking, payment, customs documentation, and return policies—before the user even reaches an agent.

Steps to launch a knowledge base

1. Identify FAQs – Pull the top 20 enquiry topics from your ticketing reports.

2. Create SEO‑friendly articles – Use the target keyword How to manage delivery enquiries for a logistics business where relevant, and include schema markup for rich snippets.

3. Host on a fast, secure platform – Use Cloudflare for CDN caching and Namecheap for domain registration (e.g., support.ai‑business.com.ng).

4. Integrate search – Implement Algolia or ElasticSearch so users can instantly find articles.

5. Add a chatbot – A bot trained on the knowledge base can suggest articles in real‑time, reducing ticket creation by up to 30%.

When a user does submit a ticket, the system can suggest the most relevant article automatically, ensuring that the customer feels assisted even before an agent replies.

Within a week you’ll have a centralized inbox, measurable response times, and the foundation for scaling your enquiry‑management process.
