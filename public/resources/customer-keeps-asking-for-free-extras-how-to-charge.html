They Keep Asking for "Just One More Thing" Free — How to Charge for Extras

"It's just a small change" is the most expensive sentence in business

Scope creep never arrives as a big request. It arrives as ten small ones — "can you also…", "while you're at it…", "it's just a tweak" — each too minor to argue about, and together they eat the margin on the whole job. The reason it works on you is that each individual ask feels rude to charge for. So the fix is not to fight each tweak; it's to make the boundary structural, before the tweaks start.

---

Stop creep at the source: define the edge in writing

The cheapest place to handle scope creep is the proposal, not the WhatsApp thread three weeks later. List what's included, and — just as important — what isn't. A line like "Includes 2 revision rounds; additional revisions or new pages are quoted separately" does the work for you every time they ask, without you ever having to say no in the moment.

When they ask for the extraSay this (warm, factual, no apology)
It's clearly outside scope"Happy to add that! It's outside what we agreed, so it's a small separate item — ₦X, and I'll slot it in this week."
It's borderline / tiny"I'll do that one free as a courtesy — for anything bigger like [the real version], I'd quote it so it's done properly."
They push ("but it's just…")"I know it looks small from your side — it's a fresh [task/test/build] on mine. That's why it's priced as its own thing."
They want it now, mid-project"Sure — let me finish what we scoped first so this stays on time, then I'll send the add-on quote right after."

---

The courtesy that prevents the resentment

Notice the second row: occasionally giving one tiny extra away, named as a courtesy, buys enormous goodwill and teaches them the boundary at the same time ("for anything bigger I'd quote it"). The mistake is giving extras silently — then they assume everything is free, and your quiet generosity becomes their expectation.

---

Bill the extras as their own line, always

The moment an extra is folded into the original invoice, it becomes invisible and unpaid-in-spirit. A separate line — or a separate small invoice — makes the creep real, makes the total honest, and makes the next "just one more thing" a conversation about money instead of a favor.
