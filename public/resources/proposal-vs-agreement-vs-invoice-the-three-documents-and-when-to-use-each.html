Proposal vs Agreement vs Invoice

One funnel, three documents

Owners mix these up and wonder why deals stall. They're not three versions of the same thing — they're three stages, and using the wrong one at the wrong time is how a yes evaporates:

DocumentJobStageThe question it answers
📄 ProposalSell the offerBefore the yes"Should I hire you?"
✍️ AgreementLock the yesRight after the yes"Are we actually doing this, on these terms?"
🧾 InvoiceGet paidDuring/after delivery"What do I owe you, and when?"

Where the money leaks

Most owners jump straight from proposal to invoice and skip the agreement entirely — they send the offer, get a verbal yes, then start working, then bill. That skipped middle is the leak. The proposal convinced them; the invoice demands money; but nothing in between held the commitment. The agreement is the hinge: it converts "sounds good" into "we have a deal, here are the terms, here's the date." Without it you're chasing a yes you never actually secured.

The order that closes

  1. Proposal — make the offer irresistible and clear.
  2. Agreement — the moment they say yes, lock the terms in writing while they're warm.
  3. Invoice — bill against the agreed terms, on the agreed schedule.

Get the sequence right and each document does its one job, and the deal moves forward instead of stalling in the gap between "yes" and "paid."
