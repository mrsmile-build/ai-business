Job or Business? Choose by Runway, Not by Dream

The two risks, named plainly

A job is someone else carrying the risk and paying you a steady slice for your time. A business is you carrying the risk, with no guaranteed slice but no ceiling either. Neither is noble or shameful; they're just different bets. The mistake is choosing by mood ("I'm tired of bosses") instead of by runway — how many months you can eat without income.

The runway rule

Your runwayThe sane move
0–1 month of food/rentTake income first — job or paid skill work. A business on an empty stomach makes desperate decisions.
2–5 monthsThe hybrid: keep income, build the business in the evenings with one paying client at a time.
6+ months AND a skill people pay forYou can afford a focused business sprint — but still sell before you build.

The hybrid is not a compromise, it's the strategy

Most successful small-business owners didn't leap; they bridged. Job pays the rent; one side client at a time proves the business; when side income covers half the rent, you shift. The hybrid removes the desperation that kills early businesses, and it gives the business something a leap never has: real clients, real feedback, real proof — before you depend on it.

Whichever you choose, the first move is the same

Job: a proof-first CV and ten targeted applications. Business: one paying client and a page a stranger can check. Both are "get one person to say yes with evidence." The tools differ; the move doesn't.

Don't ask "job or business?" Ask "how many months can I eat, and who will say yes first?" The answer chooses for you — and it's usually the hybrid.
