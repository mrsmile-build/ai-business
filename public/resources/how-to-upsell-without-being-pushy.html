How to Upsell Without Being Pushy

An upsell is pushy only when it's about your quota

The reason upselling feels dirty is that most people do it wrong: they pitch more because they want more money. Customers smell that instantly. The version that works is when the extra genuinely serves the thing they already bought — you're not extracting, you're completing. The test is simple: would you recommend this add-on to a friend who asked, even if you weren't getting paid for it? If yes, say it. If no, stay quiet.

---

Three moments an upsell lands naturally

MomentWhy it worksExample line
At delivery, when they're happiestTrust is at its peak; the suggestion reads as care, not greed"Glad you love the [main thing]. A lot of clients add [related thing] next so they don't have to [pain it solves] — want me to size that for you?"
When they hit a limit you can seeYou're solving a problem they already feel, not creating one"You're filling this up fast — if you add [capacity/feature] now it'll save you a restart later."
On a repeat orderLoyalty is the safest time to expand"Since you're reordering, the bundle version works out cheaper per use — shall I switch you?"

---

The framing that removes the push

  • Offer, don't pitch. One sentence, then stop. Let them decide in silence.
  • Tie it to their outcome, not your price. "So you don't have to do X again" beats "it's only ₦Y more."
  • Make the default the original. They keep what they bought unless they actively choose more. No pre-ticked addons, no pressure.
  • Accept a no gracefully and once. A respected "no" sells the next upsell better than any pitch.

---

Track what they actually say yes to

Over time you'll notice the same add-ons get accepted by similar customers. That pattern is gold — it tells you which bundles to offer first, and which to stop wasting breath on. But only if you remember who bought what across visits, which is the thing owners reliably forget.
