A Written Agreement Protects the Friendship

The deals that hurt most are the warm ones

Strangers who stiff you are a business loss. Friends who stiff you are a life loss — because the money and the relationship get tangled, and suddenly you can't tell if you're owed naira or owed respect. The informal deal with someone you love feels generous in the moment ("of course I'll sort you out") and becomes resentment the day expectations diverge.

Clarity is kindness, not coldness

The paradox nobody expects: writing it down saves the friendship, because it removes the ambiguity that breeds the quiet anger. When both people can see the same terms, there's nothing to misremember, nothing to feel short-changed, nothing to poison the relationship later. The paper isn't a wall between you — it's a fence that keeps the garden safe.

How to raise it with someone you love

Frame it exactly as it is: "Because you matter to me, I want this clear so it never comes between us." That sentence does the heavy lifting — it tells them the agreement is an act of care for the relationship, not a lack of trust in the person. Most friends, honestly told, are relieved. They've been quietly anxious about the same ambiguity.

The friend you lose over a clear agreement is the friend you would have lost anyway — slower, and with more pain — over a vague one.
