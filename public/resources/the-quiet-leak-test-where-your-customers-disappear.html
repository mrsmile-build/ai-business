The Quiet-Leak Test: 3 questions to find where your customers disappear

Everyone tells you the fix for a slow month is more leads. Sometimes it is. But more often the bucket already has water in it - customers who asked, who said "send me details," who were real - and the water is leaking out the bottom while you pour more in from the top. You cannot fix a leak you cannot see. So answer three honest questions and find the exact stage where your customers vanish.

Question 1: Do you know who asked, and when?

Write down, from memory, the last five people who enquired and did not buy. If you cannot name three of them without opening a chat, that is leak number one: you are not recording the enquiry at all. A notebook helps until the page tears out or the phone dies. The fix is not "try harder to remember" - it is a system that logs who asked, what they asked for, and the date, automatically, so the list survives a busy week.

Question 2: Do you nudge the quiet ones in your own voice?

Of the people you just named, how many got a follow-up after they went silent? Not a broadcast. Not a template that sounds like a robot. A real "hey, still thinking about the X you asked about?" in the way you actually talk to that person. If the answer is "I meant to but forgot," that is leak number two: the follow-up exists in your head and dies there. The nudge has to be drafted for you and waiting, so the only thing left is for you to read it and tap send.

Question 3: Do you remember what you promised?

Think of the last customer who said "I will get back to you." Did you record what they were deciding on, and when you said you would check in? If "I will get back to you" became "never," that is leak number three: the promise evaporated because nothing held it. A quiet thread is not a lost customer yet - it is a customer waiting for you to remember them.

Reading your score

  • 0 of 3 - your leak is memory. You are losing customers to silence, not to competition. The first fix is a place that records every enquiry and flags the quiet ones.
  • 1 of 3 - you record but do not follow up. The leads are safe; the nudges are not. Add the drafted follow-up that waits for your approval.
  • 2 of 3 - you follow up but forget promises. Tie each quiet thread to what was actually discussed, so the next message sounds like you were listening.
  • 3 of 3 - your leak is not memory, it is conversion. The customers remember you; they just are not buying. That is a different problem (price, trust, timing) and worth its own diagnosis.

Notice what none of these three questions are about: writing. You can already write a message. The leak is never the words - it is the remembering, the nudging, the holding-on-to-the-promise. That is the part a notebook and a free chatbot will never do for you, because both of them forget the customer the moment you close the tab.
