The 15-Minute Weekly Review

Tactics vs strategy — you need both clocks

The 5-minute morning check keeps today from leaking. But a business isn't lost in a day — it's lost in a month of busy days that never moved the needle. That's why the weekly review exists: it's the only time you zoom out far enough to see whether you're climbing the right mountain, not just climbing fast. Fifteen minutes, same day every week. Non-negotiable, like a meeting with your most important client — you.

The four questions

QuestionWhat it catches
1. What moved money this week?The truth about what's actually working — not what felt busy.
2. What stalled, and why?The quiet leaks: the lead you never followed up, the proposal that died in silence.
3. What's the ONE thing next week?Forces the focus the scatter steals. One priority, written down.
4. What did I learn?Compounds your judgement so week 20 is smarter than week 2.

The rule that stops you quitting on a feeling

Here's where the weekly review saves relationships and businesses alike: when you're tempted to abandon a plan, the review makes you ask why before you act. Is there real evidence it's failing — empty replies, no conversions, a market that spoke? Or are you just tired, bored, or scared, and the "new shiny idea" is an escape? Switch on evidence, never on mood. A plan judged weekly gets a fair trial; a plan abandoned daily never gets one. This is the difference between pivoting and running.

Make it a page, not a thought

Write the four answers down — one page, every week, in the same place. A year of these pages is the most valuable document you'll own: it shows you, in your own handwriting, which ideas were real and which were moods. You'll stop repeating the same three mistakes, because you'll finally be able to see them.

Scatter isn't cured by working harder. It's cured by a weekly moment where you choose one thing and defend it from your own next idea.
