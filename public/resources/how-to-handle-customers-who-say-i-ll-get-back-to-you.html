How to Handle Customers Who Say “I’ll Get Back to You”

How to Deal with Customers Who Say “I’ll Get Back to You”

“I’ll get back to you”—it’s the phrase that makes salespeople’s hearts skip a beat. It sounds polite, but more often than not it’s a polite way of saying “I’m not ready yet” or “I’m not convinced.” If you’ve ever left a meeting feeling unsure whether you’ll ever hear from that prospect again, you’re not alone. In this comprehensive guide we’ll unpack the psychology behind the statement, arm you with concrete follow‑up systems, and give you an actionable plan that turns a vague promise into a closed deal.

---

1. Why “I’ll Get Back to You” Isn’t Just Politeness – It’s a Signal

Understanding the underlying motive is the first step to handling it effectively.

| Common Reason | What It Means for You |

|---------------|-----------------------|

| Need More Time | The prospect is genuinely busy. They need a clear next step to re‑engage. |

| Price Anxiety | Money is the barrier. They’ll need reassurance about ROI before committing. |

| Decision‑Making Hierarchy | They must run the idea past a boss, finance, or legal. You need to identify the true decision‑maker. |

| Lack of Urgency | The problem isn’t painful enough yet. You must heighten perceived urgency. |

| Testing the Waters | They’re gauging your responsiveness. Your follow‑up speed will set the tone for the relationship. |

Key Insight: The phrase is rarely a dead‑end; it’s a data point. Treat it as a diagnostic cue, not a rejection.

---

2. Preparing Before the Conversation – Your Arsenal of Tools

A well‑prepared salesperson can turn a vague promise into a scheduled next step before the meeting ends.

2.1. Capture Every Detail in a CRM

Use a CRM like HubSpot or Zoho to log:

  • Prospect’s name, title, and contact details
  • Decision‑making hierarchy
  • Specific objections mentioned
  • Preferred communication channel and time zone

A clean record lets you automate personalized follow‑ups without forgetting crucial context.

2.2. Automate Email Follow‑Ups

Platforms such as Mailchimp (mailchimp.com) or Sendinblue (sendinblue.com) let you schedule a sequence that:

1. Sends a thank‑you email within 15 minutes.

2. Delivers a value‑add resource (case study, ROI calculator) 24‑48 hours later.

3. Checks in with a short question after 5 days.

Automation ensures you stay top‑of‑mind without being pushy.

2.3. Use Scheduling Links to Lock the Next Call

Instead of asking “When can we talk again?”, embed a Calendly (calendly.com) or Acuity link in your follow‑up email. The prospect can pick a slot that fits their calendar, converting “I’ll get back to you” into a concrete appointment.

2.4. Leverage Payment Gateways for Quick Wins

If the prospect is close to a purchase, streamline the checkout with local payment providers:

  • Paystack – https://paystack.com/
  • Flutterwave – https://flutterwave.com/

A frictionless payment experience can tip the scales from maybe to yes.

3. Proven Follow‑Up Frameworks That Convert

Research shows that the average sales professional makes only 2–3 touches before a lead goes cold. Successful teams use systematic frameworks to out‑perform that baseline.

3.1. The 3‑Touch, 48‑Hour Rule

1. Immediate Thank‑You (0‑15 min) – Reinforce the value you discussed.

2. Resource Drop (24 hrs) – Share a relevant case study, ROI sheet, or demo video.

3. Decision‑Prompt (48 hrs) – Ask a concise question that forces a response, e.g., “Which of these two pricing options aligns best with your budget?”

Statistically, this cadence lifts reply rates by 30‑45 %.

3.2. The “Problem‑Agitate‑Solution” Follow‑Up

  • Problem – Remind them of the pain point you uncovered.
  • Agitate – Quantify the cost of inaction (lost revenue, time, compliance risk).
  • Solution – Show how your product resolves it, backed by numbers.

Example email snippet:

> “During our call you mentioned that manual invoicing is costing your finance team about 12 hours per week. That’s roughly ₦1.2 M in lost productivity each month. Our automated billing platform reduces that effort by 85 %, saving you ₦1 M annually.”

3.3. The “Closed‑Loop” Question

Instead of the generic “Let me know when you’re ready”, ask a question that requires a yes/no answer and a next step:

  • “Would Thursday at 10 am work for a quick demo of the integration you’re interested in?”
  • “Can I send over a draft contract for your legal team to review?”

A closed‑loop question forces the prospect to either accept or provide an alternative, preventing the conversation from stalling.

---

4. Psychological Nudges That Turn “Maybe” Into “Yes”

People are wired to respond to scarcity, social proof, and commitment cues. Use these ethically to push the prospect forward.

4.1. Scarcity & Deadline

“We have 5 slots left for onboarding this quarter. If we start before the 30th, we can guarantee the promotional discount.”

The fear of missing out (FOMO) creates urgency without sounding aggressive.

4.2. Social Proof

Share a mini‑testimonial from a client in the same industry. Include metrics:

> “Acme Corp reduced churn by 27 % after implementing our solution – see the full case study here.”

Seeing peers succeed reduces perceived risk.

4.3. Commitment & Consistency

Get a small, low‑friction commitment early – a “yes” to a quick 5‑minute discovery call or a request for a product brochure. According to Cialdini’s principle of consistency, once someone says yes to a small ask, they are more likely to say yes to a larger one later.

5. Actionable Takeaway – Your 7‑Day “Get‑Back‑to‑You” Playbook

| Day | Action | Tool |

|-----|--------|------|

| Day 0 | Send personalized thank‑you email (include Calendly link) | Gmail + Calendly |

| Day 1 | Deliver a relevant case study or ROI calculator | Google Drive (share link) |

| Day 2 | Follow‑up with a “Problem‑Agitate‑Solution” email + closed‑loop question | Mailchimp automation |

| Day 3 | If no response, add a scarcity line (limited slots, discount deadline) | HubSpot task reminder |

| Day 4 | Share a short 2‑minute testimonial video from a similar client | Vimeo or YouTube embed |

| Day 5 | Call the prospect – reference the previous emails and ask for a final decision | Phone/Zoom |

| Day 6 | If still silent, send a “Last‑Chance” email with a clear CTA to schedule or decline | Sendinblue sequence |

| Day 7 | Log outcome in CRM, move to “Closed‑Lost” or “Won” pipeline | HubSpot CRM |

Follow this playbook for every prospect who says “I’ll get back to you.” Consistency breeds predictability, and predictability leads to higher close rates.
