How to Know Which Leads Are Ready to Buy – Proven Strategies

How to Know Which Leads Are Ready to Buy

In a world where every click could be a potential customer, knowing which leads are truly ready to buy is the difference between wasted effort and a thriving revenue pipeline.

---

1. Understanding Lead Intent – The Foundation of Qualification

Before you can label a prospect as “ready,” you need to understand lead intent. Intent is the combination of a prospect’s need, budget, authority, and timing (often abbreviated as BANT). When these four pillars align, the likelihood of a purchase skyrockets.

1.1. Need vs. Want

  • Need: A problem that is causing pain or loss. Example: A Nigerian retailer struggling with manual payment reconciliation.
  • Want: A nice‑to‑have feature that improves efficiency but isn’t critical.

Leads expressing a need are typically further along the funnel. Ask open‑ended questions during discovery calls: “What happens if you don’t solve this problem in the next 30 days?” The urgency in their answer often reveals readiness.

1.2. Budget Signals

  • Explicit budget disclosures (e.g., “We have a ₦5,000,000 allocation”) are rare but gold.
  • Implicit cues such as asking about pricing tiers, payment plans, or integration costs often indicate that the prospect is already budgeting for a solution.

1.3. Authority & Decision‑Making Power

Identify the Economic Buyer (the person who signs the cheque) and the Technical Buyer (the person who approves integration). If a lead can introduce you to both, you’re moving past the gatekeeper stage.

1.4. Timing – The Purchase Window

A lead who says, “We need to be live by the end of Q4,” provides a clear deadline. Align your sales cadence to that timeline and watch the conversion rate climb.

Key Takeaway: Mapping each lead against BANT creates a baseline qualification that separates cold curiosity from genuine buying intent.

---

2. Lead Scoring Models that Work – Quantify Readiness

Human intuition is powerful, but a data‑driven lead scoring system removes guesswork and scales qualification across thousands of prospects.

2.1. Demographic Scoring

Assign points for:

  • Company size (e.g., >50 employees = 10 points)
  • Industry relevance (e.g., fintech, e‑commerce = 8 points)
  • Geography (Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya = 5 points)

2.2. Behavioral Scoring

Track actions that signal intent:

  • Visiting pricing page → +15 points
  • Downloading a case study → +10 points
  • Requesting a demo → +20 points
  • Engaging with email (open + click) → +5 points per interaction

2.3. Predictive Scoring with AI

Tools like HubSpot’s predictive lead scoring or Salesforce Einstein use machine learning to weigh dozens of variables automatically. If you’re a lean startup, consider open‑source libraries such as scikit‑learn to build a custom model.

2.4. Setting the Threshold

A common practice is to set a score of 70+ (out of 100) as “sales‑ready.” Leads below that become nurture candidates, receiving drip campaigns until they cross the line.

2.5. Continuous Optimization

Every closed‑won deal should be fed back into the scoring algorithm. Over time, the model learns which signals truly predict purchase.

Actionable Takeaway: Implement a simple 0‑100 lead score in your CRM. When a lead hits 70, automatically assign it to a sales rep and trigger a personalized outreach sequence.

---

3. Behavioral Signals & Real‑Time Triggers – Spot the Hot Leads Instantly

In the digital age, prospects leave a breadcrumb trail. By monitoring real‑time behavioral triggers, you can strike while the iron is hot.

3.1. Website Activity

  • Page depth: 3+ internal pages visited in a single session suggests deeper research.
  • Session duration: >5 minutes on your pricing or integration pages is a strong intent indicator.
  • Exit intent: If a visitor tries to leave after viewing a demo video, pop up a chat widget offering a live demo.

3.2. Email Engagement

  • Multiple opens of a product email → indicates high interest.
  • Click‑throughs to a booking calendar (e.g., Calendly) → a clear buying signal.

3.3. Social Signals

  • LinkedIn comments on your product post.
  • Twitter mentions asking about pricing.

3.4. Payment Platform Interactions

If a prospect has already set up a Paystack (https://paystack.com/ or Flutterwave (https://flutterwave.com/ sandbox account, they’re likely testing integration – a strong purchase indicator.

3.5. Real‑Time Alerts via Automation

Use tools like Zapier or Make (Integromat) to push alerts to Slack or your CRM when a lead meets any of the above criteria. Immediate notification allows sales to act within minutes, dramatically increasing conversion probability.

5. Turning Insights into Action – The Final Playbook

All the data in the world is useless without a clear execution plan. Here’s a step‑by‑step playbook you can implement this week:

1. Map BANT criteria for your ideal customer profile (ICP) in your CRM.

2. Set up lead scoring: assign points for demographic and behavioral actions.

3. Create real‑time alerts: use Zapier to push a Slack notification when a lead scores >70 or visits the pricing page.

4. Automate outreach: a personalized email sequence is triggered automatically for scores 50‑69, while a sales rep receives an instant task for scores 70+.

5. Monitor performance: review weekly dashboards to see conversion rates at each score tier and adjust point values accordingly.

6. Iterate: feed every closed‑won and lost deal back into your scoring model to refine accuracy.

Actionable Takeaway: Implement the above six‑step workflow in the next 7 days, and you should see a 20‑30% increase in qualified‑lead conversion within the first month.
