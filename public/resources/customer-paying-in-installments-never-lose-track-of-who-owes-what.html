Customer Paying in Installments? Never Lose Track of Who Owes What

Installments are trust, not weakness

Letting a good customer pay in three is often the difference between a yes and a no. The risk isn't the installments — it's the memory. By week three, "she paid half, I think" is how a fair arrangement becomes an awkward conversation, and sometimes a lost friendship.

The three numbers that matter

NumberRule
Total agreedWritten down before work starts — never reconstructed from memory.
Paid so farEvery payment logged the day it lands, with the date.
Next dueAmount and date of the next installment — set the reminder now, not later.

The reminder that never feels like chasing

Send it before the due date, framed as helpfulness: "Quick heads-up, your second installment is due Friday — want me to send the link?" A reminder sent early is service. The same words sent after the date feel like debt collection. Same message, different timing, opposite feeling.
